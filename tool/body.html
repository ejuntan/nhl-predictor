What this model can and can't do

It is honest about game winners but not sharp. 54.8% accuracy on 1,417 games it had never seen, which beats goal-differential Elo and the standings. Against real sportsbook prices on those same games it is statistically level on log loss but worse calibrated, it wins under half the games where it disagrees with the market on which side to take, and betting its picks at those prices lost 8.1% per dollar — its value picks lost 2.6%, which is the vig and nothing more.

The goalie matters less than it looks. Swapping a guessed starter for the confirmed one changes the model's predictions by almost nothing. Which goalie plays turns out to predict how he plays barely at all — so the flags below are honest about the guess, but the guess costs little.

Shots on goal is the good part. It removes 13% of the error a league-average guess would make, against 2.4% for game winners, and beats a player's own recent average across 47,000 skater-games.

Goal totals and the overtime price are not shown. Both are right on average and neither moves with the game. The expected total explains 0.33% of the variance in real totals, its estimates span 5.6 to 7.6 while real games run from 1 to 16, and on the games where it disagrees with a sportsbook's posted total it is right 51.2% of the time against the 52.4% a −110 price needs. The overtime price scores slightly worse than simply quoting the league's 24.5%. A number on a card implies it tells you something about that game, so these two were taken off rather than labelled. The sportsbook's own total is no better than the league average either, which is why this reads as a property of hockey rather than a fault here. Finished games still show what was predicted against what happened.

Goalies are a guess. The backtest knew who started; this page does not. On a back-to-back it assumes the backup, marked with a flag. A wrong goalie moves a win probability more than anything else here.

Lineups are last game's. A skater appears if he played his team's previous game, so same-day scratches and call-ups are missed, and expected ice time is his own recent average.

Props carry more vig. Books charge roughly twice as much on a shots prop as on a moneyline, so a one-percent model edge does not clear a six-percent hold. An edge here has to be large before it means anything.
